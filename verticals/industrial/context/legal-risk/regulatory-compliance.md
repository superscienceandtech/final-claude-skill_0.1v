## Regulators that arrive at the site

A cross-industry compliance program is built around obligations that arrive as filings. An operating
site adds regulators who arrive in person, inspect equipment and interview employees, and whose
obligations run on clocks measured in hours.

The categories worth tracking separately, because each has its own trigger and its own owner:

- **Occupational safety.** Recordkeeping is continuous and the log is posted annually; serious
  events carry a short notification window. The inspection is unannounced and the walkthrough is
  itself evidence. `operations:ehs-and-workplace-safety` holds the operational side.
- **Environmental permits.** Air, wastewater, stormwater and waste each carry conditions, monitoring
  and periodic reporting. A permit condition breached is a violation whether or not anything was
  released, and permits expire on their own schedule rather than the company's.
- **Release reporting.** Spills and releases above a reportable quantity trigger notification within
  hours. Knowing the quantities for what is on site is preparation, not response — nobody derives
  them correctly at the time.
- **Product and material regulation.** Hazard communication and safety data sheets, restricted
  substances, labeling, and anything specific to what the site makes or handles.
- **Equipment certification.** Pressure vessels, lifting equipment, forklifts and their operators
  carry inspection and certification intervals with their own dates.

**A site has a finite, enumerable obligation list, and most sites have never written it down.**
Producing it once — obligation, regulator, trigger, frequency, named owner, evidence location — is
the highest-value compliance work an operating site can do, and it is usually assembled in a week
from permits already in the file.

Contractors and temporary workers on the site are inside most of these obligations and outside most
companies' tracking of them.
